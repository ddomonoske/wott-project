\section{Cornering Model}
\label{sec:cornering-model}

When the rider's center-of-mass height $h$ (\texttt{com\_height\_m}) and the
track's curvature profile $\kappa(s)$ (\cref{sec:track-geometry}) are both known,
the simulation adds a cornering correction to the equation of motion. This
section derives that correction term by term.

\subsection{Framing: a quasi-static lean, not a dynamical degree of freedom}
\label{sec:quasi-static-lean}

Before deriving the correction, it is worth stating precisely what kind of model
this is, since it is easy to over-read the following equations as a full
rigid-body treatment. The lean angle $\theta$ is \emph{algebraically slaved} to
the instantaneous speed $v$ and track curvature $\kappa$ at every instant; it is
not an independent state variable with its own rotational inertia, and the model
does not track a separate roll rate. There is also no friction-circle or
traction-limit check: $\theta$ is computed unconditionally from $v$ and
$\kappa$ regardless of whether the tire could actually supply the implied
lateral friction. This is a deliberate simplification, revisited in
\cref{sec:assumptions}, appropriate for the smooth, moderate-curvature track
geometry of \cref{sec:track-geometry} but not a general-purpose vehicle dynamics
model.

\subsection{Lean angle}
\label{sec:lean-angle}

For a bicycle in a steady, flat turn of radius $r = 1/\kappa$ at speed $v$, the
classic no-slip lean-angle balance equates the lateral force required for
circular motion, $m v^2 / r$, with the horizontal component of gravity acting
through the lean:
\begin{equation}
    \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g r} = \frac{v^2 \kappa}{g}.
    \label{eq:lean-angle-tan}
\end{equation}
Solving for $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
    \theta(v,\kappa) = \arctan\!\left(\frac{v^2 \kappa}{g}\right).
    \label{eq:lean-angle}
\end{equation}
On a straight ($\kappa = 0$) or at rest ($v=0$), $\theta = 0$ and every term
introduced in this section vanishes, so the model reduces exactly to
\cref{sec:simulation-model} with no cornering configured.

\subsection{Center-of-mass coning and wheel-speed geometry}
\label{sec:coning}

The state variable $v$ represents the speed of the rider's center of mass. When
leaned at angle $\theta$, the center of mass sits a horizontal distance
$h \sin\theta$ inboard of the wheel-ground contact line, tracing a smaller
effective turn radius than the wheels themselves, which remain on the track
surface. For the same angular sweep of the turn, the wheels -- farther from the
turn center -- must therefore cover more ground per unit time than the center of
mass. Writing this as a scaling of the center-of-mass speed,
\begin{equation}
    v_{\mathrm{wheel}}(v, \kappa, \theta) =
        \frac{v}{1 - \kappa h \sin\theta}.
    \label{eq:wheel-speed}
\end{equation}
This matters because track curvature $\kappa(s)$ is indexed by arc length along
the track surface -- the wheels' path -- not by the center of mass's path.
Consequently the position state advances at the wheel speed rather than the
center-of-mass speed,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{ds}{dt} = v_{\mathrm{wheel}},
    \label{eq:ds-dt}
\end{equation}
so that curvature lookups $\kappa(s)$ stay correctly aligned with the track
geometry the wheels are actually traveling over. This is a purely geometric
correction; it carries no direct effect on $dv/dt$.

\subsection{Energy-feedback term}
\label{sec:energy-feedback}

As the rider leans into and out of a corner, the center of mass also moves
\emph{vertically}: its height above the ground is proportional to $h\cos\theta$,
which decreases as $\theta$ grows from zero. By conservation of mechanical
energy, this vertical motion trades against forward kinetic energy -- leaning
into a corner draws a small amount of speed away from forward motion, and
leaning back out returns it. This subsection derives the resulting correction to
$dv/dt$.

\subsubsection{Explicit s-dependence of theta}

Differentiating \cref{eq:lean-angle-tan} with respect to arc length $s$ at fixed
$v$, using $x \equiv v^2\kappa/g$ and $\frac{d}{ds}\arctan(x) = \frac{1}{1+x^2}\frac{dx}{ds}$,
gives the rate of change of lean angle due to the changing curvature alone:
\begin{equation}
    \left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial s}\right|_{v} =
        \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,\frac{v^2}{g}\,\frac{d\kappa}{ds}.
    \label{eq:dtheta-ds}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Vertical-energy acceleration term}

The center-of-mass height above a fixed reference is, up to a constant,
$h\cos\theta$, so its vertical velocity is
$\frac{dz}{dt} = -h\sin\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}$. Energy conservation on the
combined kinetic and potential terms, $\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}v^2 + gz\right) = 0$
for the lean-driven exchange alone, gives $v\,\frac{dv}{dt}\Big|_{\text{lean}} = gh\sin\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}$,
or
\begin{equation}
    a_{\mathrm{energy}} = \frac{gh\sin\theta}{v}\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}.
    \label{eq:a-energy-general}
\end{equation}
Only the arc-length-driven part of $d\theta/dt$ -- that is,
$\left.\partial\theta/\partial s\right|_v \cdot ds/dt$, using the wheel speed
of \cref{eq:ds-dt} -- is applied explicitly here:
\begin{equation}
    a_{\mathrm{energy}}^{(s)} =
        \frac{gh\sin\theta}{v}\left(\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial s}\right)v_{\mathrm{wheel}}.
    \label{eq:a-energy-s}
\end{equation}
The remaining part of $d\theta/dt$, driven by $v$ itself changing, is handled
separately in the next subsection: since $\theta$ depends on $v$
(\cref{eq:lean-angle}), and $v$ is precisely the quantity being solved for,
folding that dependence directly into \cref{eq:a-energy-general} would make the
right-hand side of the ODE depend on $dv/dt$ itself.

\subsubsection{Implicit feedback coefficient}

Differentiating \cref{eq:lean-angle-tan} with respect to $v$ at fixed $\kappa$
gives the $v$-driven part of $d\theta/dt$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\theta}{\partial v} =
        \frac{1}{1+x^2}\cdot\frac{2v\kappa}{g}.
    \label{eq:dtheta-dv}
\end{equation}
Substituting the full chain rule
$d\theta/dt = \left.\partial\theta/\partial s\right|_v v_{\mathrm{wheel}} + \partial\theta/\partial v \cdot dv/dt$
into \cref{eq:a-energy-general} and collecting the $dv/dt$ terms gives an
equation of the form $dv/dt = a_{\mathrm{energy}}^{(s)} + C \cdot dv/dt$ (plus the
non-cornering forces), where
\begin{equation}
    C(v,\kappa,\theta,h) =
        \frac{2 h \kappa \sin\theta}{1+x^2}.
    \label{eq:feedback-coeff}
\end{equation}
Here every explicit factor of $v$ and $g$ introduced by the
$gh\sin\theta/v$ prefactor of \cref{eq:a-energy-general} and the $2v\kappa/g$
factor of \cref{eq:dtheta-dv} cancels, leaving $C$ independent of both. Solving
the resulting equation explicitly for $dv/dt$, rather than treating $C$ as a
second implicit unknown requiring its own root-find at every solver step, gives
the combined result.

\subsection{Combined equation of motion}
\label{sec:combined-eom}

Substituting \cref{eq:a-energy-s,eq:feedback-coeff} into
\cref{eq:a-power} and solving for $dv/dt$ gives the full cornering-corrected
equation of motion:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dv}{dt} =
        \frac{a_{\mathrm{power}} + a_{\mathrm{energy}}^{(s)}}
             {\max\bigl(1 - C,\ 0.1\bigr)}.
    \label{eq:dv-dt-full}
\end{equation}
The floor on the denominator is a numerical safeguard against $C$ approaching
$1$, which would only occur at combinations of lean, curvature, and speed far
outside any realistic riding condition; it has no physical interpretation. Together
with \cref{eq:ds-dt}, \cref{eq:dv-dt-full} completes the right-hand side of the
ODE integrated in \cref{sec:numerical-integration}.

As a consistency check, this construction is antisymmetric between corner entry
and corner exit: because $d\kappa/ds$ changes sign between the entry and exit
transition ramps of \cref{sec:track-geometry} while $v$ and $\theta$ are
approximately unchanged, $a_{\mathrm{energy}}^{(s)}$ takes equal and opposite
values entering and exiting a corner at the same speed. Over a full lean-in and
lean-out cycle at constant $v$, the term therefore integrates to zero net effect
on speed, consistent with energy conservation: the center of mass returns to its
original height, so no net kinetic energy should be gained or lost.

\subsection{Low-speed floor}
\label{sec:v-eps}

All cornering and energy terms in this section are set to zero whenever
$v < V_{\mathrm{eps}}$ (\SI{0.1}{\metre\per\second}), which both avoids
division-by-near-zero in \cref{eq:a-energy-general,eq:feedback-coeff} and
reflects the fact that lean is already negligible at near-zero speed. This is a
numerical safeguard for the simulation's start condition, not a distinct
physical regime.
